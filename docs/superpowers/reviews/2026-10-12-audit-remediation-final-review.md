## F1 — HIGH: repeated recovery cycles can permanently favor the same four calls in a group

**Locations:** `signaling-actors/src/main/java/io/webrtc/signaling/actors/call/CallRecoveryService.java:254-265`, `:301-334`; `signaling-actors/src/main/java/io/webrtc/signaling/actors/call/CallActor.java:481`. Affects A01/A09, remediation Task 3, spec §§45.5/46.3/47.5 and the oldest-unreconciled-age requirement.

The durable page is ordered by `(ownership_hash_version, ownership_group_id, call_id)`. `dispatch` counts each visited hint as processed even when `handoff` refuses it because the four-per-group or 128-global wake cap is full. A completed page/cycle advances or resets the cursor using those visited rows. There is no retained fairness position for the rejected group suffix. On the next finite cycle, the same sorted prefix wins again.

Concrete legal scenario: a healthy owned group contains at least five nonterminal calls. Its first four wake operations remain physically pending while the synchronous loop visits the group's remaining hints, but settle before the next 100 ms scan. Each cycle admits the same first four calls and skips the fifth. Existing healthy actors do not make those first four wake operations synchronous no-ops: `CallActor.start` submits `backend.load` for every `WakeCall`. Keep this timing relationship for subsequent cycles and a silently stopped fifth actor is never woken by this scanner. The failure needs neither lost physical receipts nor DB overload. Other groups can continue; fresh root/call authority checks remain intact. At the global cap, the same ordering problem can favor earlier groups as well.

This is stronger than a missing benchmark: the source permits unbounded repair starvation under finite, successfully completed wake operations. Expiring participant authority can eventually terminate affected work safely, but that is not recovery liveness. The cycle timestamp can keep advancing while these skipped calls have never received a wake, so nominal scan duration cannot establish their oldest-unreconciled age.

**Correction:** retain a bounded fair continuation/rotation for groups and eligible call positions across cycles, including quota-refused hints. A completed prefix must not repeatedly reacquire all available group credits ahead of never-admitted suffix work. Keep the scanner independent of unresolved wake cleanup, the existing count/byte/physical owners, and fresh authority checks. Do not fix this by blocking the whole scanner at a saturated group, dropping original UNKNOWN owners, or adding an unbounded per-call backlog.

### A09 structural cadence conclusion

The original hard `512 rows / second`, approximately 117-second earliest last-page bound for 60k calls, **is removed**. Current source permits up to 32 pages of 512 per invocation within the original two-second budget (`CallRecoveryService.java:177,:233`), schedules native scans every 100 ms (`NativeActorMaintenance.java:53-57`), and selects an Up-host group partition plus actual locally owned groups (`NativeActorComposition.java:374-388`). SQL scanner physical ownership is separate from the at-most-128 outstanding wakes/four-per-group.

Those changes remove the old arithmetic cap; they do not prove full reconciliation in five seconds. F1 prevents that source acceptance today. Even after F1, page SQL cost, group skew, competing RECOVERY work, wake admission/latency, actor hydration, participant slack and progress at 60k calls/P2 need measurement. No achieved row rate, hydration throughput, five-second sweep, or 30-second fault recovery is asserted here.

## F2 — HIGH: fresh post-COMMIT hints can starve older durable outbox events

**Locations:** `signaling-storage/src/main/java/io/webrtc/signaling/storage/OutboxRepository.java:127-145,:173-184`; `signaling-storage/src/main/java/io/webrtc/signaling/storage/worker/OutboxDispatcher.java:111-121`; `signaling-storage/src/main/java/io/webrtc/signaling/storage/OutboxHints.java:34-43`. Affects A01/A02, Tasks 1/3 and concrete control-delivery integration, spec §46.3.

Each scan reads up to `limit` durable keyset hints, merges up to another `limit` committed hints, then claims only `limit` rows with unconditional `ORDER BY CASE WHEN event_id=ANY(preferred) THEN 0 ELSE 1 END`. The durable scan cursor has already advanced. Thus examining a durable event does not give it any share of claim service when enough preferred events are available.

Concrete native scenario: an older due PENDING event has no surviving process-local hint (for example after restart, hint overflow, or a previous unavailable delivery). Before every poll, 128 newer due events supply the 128 preferred hints. Use ACTIVE uncontended buckets, distinct destinations and completed delivery receipts so the separate delivery caps do not block progress. Every claim batch takes all 128 newer events; the older event remains PENDING even whenever its durable cursor cycles over it. A sustained stream can defer it for its entire valid retention/delivery window. The local hint queue is bounded, but bounded capacity does not prevent this strict-priority starvation. This is a source ordering scenario, not an asserted measured workload rate.

The frozen-prefix keyset repair itself remains present, and exact claim generation, worker incarnation, bucket barriers and application receipts remain valid. The new hint priority has reintroduced starvation at the next stage. The union also allows up to 256 candidate identities before the final 128 claim cap; review the combined hint/bucket-work bound against DB09 while correcting the selection.

**Correction:** give durable repair candidates a guaranteed bounded share or alternate fairly with committed hints, preserving prompt post-COMMIT wakeups, finite cursor wrap and `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`. Handle legal limit 1 explicitly without permanently excluding either source. Keep combined candidate/bucket/byte work within the intended bounds. A routing hint must not monopolize service over the persisted repair authority.
